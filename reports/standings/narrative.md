## Interpretation

**Result: parity with history, not an improvement on it.** The roster-based forecast (M1: projected player rates × projected minutes, plus goalies, no fitted coefficients except the goal-differential-to-points map) has an RMSE of 14.6 points per 82 games over 160 team-seasons (2021–2025). That is clearly better than predicting every team at the league mean (16.9, z = 4.3, ST-1 passes) and about one point worse than last season's goal differential alone (13.7; z = −1.46, inside the non-inferiority margin, so ST-2 passes). The blend with last season's goal differential (M3) is 13.9, *not* better than last season's goal differential by itself (z = −0.82, ST-3 fails). The calibration slope is 1.22 ± 0.16, just outside the pre-registered 0.8–1.2 band (ST-4 fails by 0.02; the forecast is slightly too compressed, the same direction as the team-level slope of 1.16 in the whole-model validation). The 80% intervals cover 80% (ST-5 passes). This is what §2 of the methodology expected.

**What the roster knowledge is worth.** Giving the same model the players who actually played and the minutes they actually got (the diagnostic) lowers the RMSE from 14.6 to 13.9 (z = 3.0). So opening-night uncertainty (injuries, games missed, who actually plays) costs about 0.7 points, and the model has no injury or games-played component. It still does not beat last season's goal differential even with perfect roster knowledge, which says the gap is in the team-level translation of player value (team context, coaching, goaltending deployment, luck), not only in who plays.

**Projection versus no projection.** The declared variant that uses last season's rates with no projection or aging is at 14.2, not distinguishable from M1 (M1 versus R: z = −1.29, favoring R). The results-target projection is at 15.0. As in the earlier team-level validation, the projection layer improves the player-level forecast but does not improve the team-level forecast.

**Rankings.** The rank correlation with actual points is 0.48 for M1 and 0.52 for last-season history, and each picks about two thirds of the league's top 16 (chance would be 50%). Differences of this size are within noise over five seasons.

**By season.** M1 is worst in 2021 (17.8 against 14.5 for last season's goal differential) and best in 2023 (12.4). 2021 is the first season after two shortened, unusual seasons, and the goal-differential-to-points map and the minutes targets are built from partly non-standard seasons; this was not treated differently in the pre-registration and is not adjusted for here.

**What this means for 2026-27.** A 2026-27 projection from this pipeline is worth producing as a roster-based account of *why* a team should move (aging, departures, arrivals, goalie changes), at about the accuracy of last season's results, and it should be presented that way: not as an improvement on last season's goal differential, and with the wide intervals the backtest implies (a team-season error of roughly 14 points, so most of the standings spread of about 14–19 points is not resolved). Because ST-3 failed, any blend with last season's results is a choice, not a validated improvement.

**Limitations.** Five test seasons; the opening roster is reconstructed from the first two games; no injury or games-missed model; the projection panel uses the uncalibrated total with rolling-refit persistent components, not the owner-preferred position-weighted-plus-TR total; the player values share hyperparameters fitted on all seasons; coaching and schedule are not modeled.
